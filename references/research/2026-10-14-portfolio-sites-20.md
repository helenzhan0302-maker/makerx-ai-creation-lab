# Design-Reference Analysis — 20 Portfolio Sites
### For: MakerX AI 造物实验室 (children's creative-technology course site, zh-CN, ages 9–12 + parents/teachers)

> **Companion document.** This report was produced independently and in parallel with [`2026-10-14-reference-measurements.md`](./2026-10-14-reference-measurements.md), which covers the brief's *first-priority* reference `haoqi.design` and gives a deeper `hiesther.me` reading (full `@keyframes` inventory, the four `prefers-reduced-motion` rules, the desktop/file-icon inventory). **That document is authoritative for `haoqi.design`, which is not among my 20 URLs.** The two reports agree on `hiesther.me`'s palette (`#FEFCF6` paper, `#1A1A2E` ink, yellow `#F4D758`, blue `#2B7FD8`) and on its terminal-as-hero structure; my added contribution for that site is measured first-viewport geometry, the three app tabs, the five-link ticker, and a **209-animation** count.
> Screenshots captured during this analysis are in [`screenshots/`](./screenshots/). **They are unverified** — see §3.2: my vision access failed, so nobody has looked at them.

**Method.** Every URL was fetched as text (`web_fetch`), then re-rendered in a headless Chromium via Playwright MCP and probed with `getComputedStyle` + `getBoundingClientRect` to measure: real first-viewport elements and their geometry, computed font sizes/weights/letter-spacing, background/text colour histograms, running CSS animation count, canvas/video/img counts, page height, and stack fingerprints in the served markup.
**Caveat up front:** the vision bridge was unavailable (all providers returned HTTP 401), so **I never looked at a single pixel.** All "first viewport" claims below are *measured DOM geometry and extracted text at a 1268×632 viewport*, not visual judgement. Where I infer visual feel, I say so.

**Headline result: 0 of the 20 URLs failed outright.** 8 of them were unreadable as plain text (JS shells) and required headless rendering. Details in §Failures.

## 1. Per-site findings

### 1. https://hiesther.me — ESTHER不二
- **First viewport:** A **terminal window**, not a hero image. Measured: `esther@universe ~ zsh`, `$ whoami`, `> ESTHER不二`, `$ cat about.md` → `$ open esther-os.app`, `Press Enter to Launch`, plus a live clock `22:33`. Above it, a 5-link ticker row at y=69; below it, three switcher buttons at y=581 (`01主页` / `02作品集` / `03我的OS`). No h1 text was in the first viewport at all (h1 = the SEO string "ESTHER不二：AI 时代的个人系统与 AI 协作实践", 32px).
- **Colour:** Cream `#FEFCF6` body, near-black `#1A1A2E` text. Two saturated accents only: blue `#2B7FD8` (6 bg hits) and yellow `#F4D758`. Very high key, contrast from ink-on-cream, not from colour.
- **Type:** A 4-family stack doing real work — `Noto Sans SC` (body), `Noto Serif SC` (display/serif), `Caveat` (**handwriting**), `Fira Code` (monospace/terminal). This mono-for-system + handwriting-for-human split is the typographic idea.
- **Motion:** Only `sticky` appears in the markup, yet **209 live animations** were running — this is a Vite SPA with hashed CSS, so markup-scanning badly under-reports it. Evidence of `✦` scatter glyphs repeated dozens of times in the DOM = decorative twinkle field.
- **Nav + listing:** OS metaphor. Top ticker = the project index (5 article/work links, one line each). Bottom tabs = the three top-level "apps" (home / work / my OS).
- **Adapt:** The **"personal OS" framing** — tabs as apps, a terminal block as the "about" panel, a status bar with a live clock. It maps 1:1 onto a kids' lab: *我的实验台 / 任务卡 / 作品柜* as three apps, and a "console" panel that reads like a lab instrument rather than a paragraph about yourself.
- **Don't copy:** 209 simultaneous animations and a `Press Enter to Launch` gate. For 9–12s, motion at that density is noise, and any "press to begin" gate loses a third of the class before the content appears.

### 2. https://lixiaolai.com — Reborn@xiaolai
- **First viewport:** Giant serif statement `A life-long / learner, reborn / with AI.` measured at **89.6px / weight 400 / −1.79px tracking / 89.6px line-height** (i.e. locked 1.0 leading), 552×269, with a 200×117 inline SVG beneath and a `Reading / Writing / Shipping / Coding` status block at the fold.
- **Colour:** Near-white `oklch(0.985 0.001 250)`, ink `oklch(0.2 0.003 250)`, and a single blue accent (there is a `--accent: #3a52b8` in the map SVG). Essentially **one accent total**.
- **Type:** Explicit font trio, and it *tells you*: footer reads "Set in _Newsreader_, Noto Serif & Inter". Newsreader serif display + Inter UI + JetBrains Mono labels. **Bilingual by design**: `中文 · English`, two separate RSS feeds (`/articles/rss.xml`, `/articles/rss.zh.xml`).
- **Motion:** The most restrained site in the set — **3 animations total**, and they are CSS keyframes with names in the markup: `ticker` and `ampersand-twinkle`. No GSAP, no Lenis, no canvas. A "← scroll · 14 spines" horizontal book shelf.
- **Nav + listing:** Numbered editorial scroll: `§ 01 · ARTICLES`, `§ 02 · BOOKS`, plus a numbered nav (`01Articles 02Books 04News 05About`). Article cards = image + date + title, four per row. Books = a horizontal spine shelf with `01`–`14` index numbers and bilingual titles.
- **Adapt:** **Numbered sections as a reading spine** (`§01 §02 §03`) plus the **colophon habit** — the page states its own typesetting ("SET IN Newsreader, NOTO SERIF & INTER · 14 OCT 2026") and its own cadence ("~2 articles / week", "14 volumes, in rotation"). For a lab site, a footer line like *"本期 6 个任务 · 3 个作品上架 · 材料包每周三更新"* makes the site feel alive and maintained, which is exactly what makes parents trust a course.
- **Don't copy:** The literary-serif register. At 89.6px with 1.0 line-height and a serif at weight 400, this reads as an essayist's journal. A kids' lab needs heavier, rounder, more forgiving letterforms and looser leading.

### 3. https://www.mantis.works — Mantis
- **First viewport:** A **full-bleed 1280×800 `<canvas>` at y=0**, six `svg` copies of the 134×32 logo, an h1 at 85.03px/−4.25px tracking, and a grid of floating media tiles (453×440 img, 412×400 img, 412×400 `<video>` `.mov`, etc.). So: 3D/canvas background + floating video objects + one big line of type.
- **Colour:** Mid-grey `#BDBDBD` body with `#252525` ink and 23 blocks at `#CFCFCF` — plus one single `rgb(0,255,0)` hit. A near-monochrome studio palette; the colour comes from the media, not the CSS.
- **Type:** Two custom faces only: `Denim-…` (display, 85px/−4.25px) and `Graebenbach Mono-…` (labels). Weight 500 display, uppercase mono nav.
- **Motion:** `lenis` smooth-scroll + `mix-blend`; **6 canvases and 6 videos** across 12,690px. A noise overlay keyframe literally named `noiseAnimation`. A live NYC clock in the chrome (`New York City 10:37:24`) that I watched tick between two probes.
- **Nav + listing:** 2-item nav (`Our Services`, `Contact Us`) that renders its label twice ("ServicesServices") — the classic hover-duplicate marquee technique. Work listing = image card + title + 2 tags (`Experiential`, `Event Production`), then a "Up Next" footer link to the next case study.
- **Adapt:** The work card is the cleanest metadata shape in the whole set: **image → project name → two topic tags.** For a kids' lab: thumbnail → 项目名 → 两个标签 (`电路` `纸模`). Children can scan it; parents can see what category of skill it teaches.
- **Don't copy:** Case-study body copy is **revealed on scroll and invisible before it** — my probe read `scrollHeight: 13132` but extracted almost nothing but the chrome. Content that does not exist for a reader who hasn't scrolled is a bad deal for a course site (and terrible for a parent skimming on a phone).

### 4. https://www.brober.xyz — Bethany Robertson
- **First viewport (home):** Not a portfolio hero at all — a **"Say Hi" contact/chat panel**: h1 "Say Hi" at 48px in a *monospace* face, email/LinkedIn/GitHub list, then Email/Subject/Message/Send Message fields.
- **Colour:** Flat `#F0F0F0` grey, with an unusual **teal-ink text colour `#164F64`** and `#012B31` for emphasis. One accent, used as the text colour rather than a highlight.
- **Type:** **Two families, both Vercel's**: `GeistMonoVF` (headings — h1 is mono, 48px, −1.2px) and `GeistVF` (body). And they print their colophon: *"Colophon: Geist Mono and Geist Sans. By Vercel."*
- **Motion:** Almost none — **4 animations**, only `backdrop-filter` in the markup. The restraint is the point.
- **Nav + listing (`/work`):** Compact 4-card case-study index: **tag → title → one-line description → "See more"**. Cards: `Design / Brand / Direct Express / "0->1 product design and brand for a federal benefits banking platform"`. Nav is 3 items + a `Toggle theme` control + a `Chat with me` entry, and the site is literally navigated by an AI assistant (`broberbot: An AI Portfolio Navigator`).
- **Adapt:** **"One project = one line"** card copy. Every card answers *what it is* and *why it mattered* in a single sentence. For parents evaluating a course, that single line is worth more than a paragraph; for kids it's a readable promise.
- **Don't copy:** The homepage-as-contact-form. A course site's homepage must show the *work and tasks*; making the first screen a "say hi" form asks the visitor to have already decided.

### 5. https://justinesoulie.fr — Justine Soulié
- **First viewport:** A **full-bleed 1268×632 `<canvas>` covering the entire viewport**, with only two text links surviving measurement: `JUSTINE SOULIÉ` at top-left (15px) and `ABOUT` at top-right — both in grey `#8C8080`. Nothing else. The work is the picture.
- **Colour:** `#F4F0ED` warm off-white with large `#FFDEB4` peach blocks (4 bg hits). Black text. Two colours, extremely airy.
- **Type:** One family, `Inter`, at small sizes (15px links). Type is deliberately subordinate to illustration.
- **Motion:** `barba` (page transitions) + a custom cursor (`cursor:` in the served CSS) + a full-viewport canvas (almost certainly an image-trail effect). Zero CSS keyframe animations.
- **Nav + listing:** The most radical pattern here: the portfolio is an **8-item text list with no thumbnails** — `Hermes`, `extra`, `typography`, `miscellaneous`, `M - Lettre Infinie`, `Ponpon Mania`, `Pop Art Car`, `House of Frog`. Case study = the canvas.
- **Adapt:** **Let the artwork be the interface.** For 9–12s, a project named `House of Frog` that you *click and it fills the screen* is far more inviting than a metadata card. Use a text list of playful project names as the index, and give the canvas to the kid's own creation.
- **Don't copy:** A canvas that covers 100% of the first viewport with no readable hero. It is beautiful and it is inaccessible: no text, no contrast fallback, nothing for a screen reader, and on a school tablet it is a blank box until the WebGL context is granted.

### 6. https://www.aaabadcode.com — Toukoum
- **First viewport:** Compact SPA hero — `Hey, I'm Aaaaby 👋` as h2, `AI Engineer` as h1 at **72px/700**, a `landing-memojis.png` sprite image, and a horizontal tab strip: `Me | Projects | Skills | Fun | Contact`.
- **Colour:** Pure white `oklch(1 0 0)` with near-black ink; a single translucent white glass layer (`oklab(… / 0.3)`). Effectively monochrome.
- **Type:** System sans (`ui-sans-serif`) at 72px/700 for the h1 and 16px body — hierarchy by size only, no custom font at all.
- **Motion:** Exactly **3 animations** and only `prefers-reduced-motion` in the markup. A `<canvas>` is present (1) but the page is 770px tall — a single screen, no scroll storytelling.
- **Nav + listing:** **Tab-based sections**, not scroll. `Me / Projects / Skills / Fun / Contact` swap content in place.
- **Adapt:** The **`Fun` tab.** A course site that admits one whole section is just for fun (doodles, fails, jokes, bloopers) is telling 9–12s the truth about making things — and it buys credibility that a purely achievement-toned site never gets. Also: tabs mean *nothing is hidden below the fold*.
- **Don't copy:** Letting it be a one-screen dead end. With no scroll and no visible project grid, a visitor can't tell there is depth. A kids' lab should show at least the top edge of the first three task cards.

### 7. https://majd-portfolio.framer.website — Majd
- **First viewport (measured, 1268×632):** Giant headline `SOFTWARE ENGINEER` at **120px / weight 800 / −2.4px tracking**, box 840×216 at (207, 208) — and **three free-floating image objects around it**: 124×124 at (163, 139) top-left, 220×251 at (517, 361) centre (alt: *"Portrait of portfolio creator"*), 173×173 at (941, 348) right. Plus a giant counter `©2026` at **58px / 600** bottom-left at (207, 554) and `/CREATING SINCE 2020` at 18px.
- **Colour:** Cream `#FAF7F3` with **large fully-dark `#111` blocks** (39 hits) — a hard light/dark alternation per section, and nothing in between. Ink is `#111`.
- **Type:** `Archivo` at weights up to 800, tight negative tracking on display, plus a mono-ish `/CREATING SINCE 2020` label register. (The measured 12px body is Framer's default root size — not an intentional design value.)
- **Motion:** Framer-built (`data-framer` + `framer` in markup) + **lenis** smooth scroll. 12 animations, `sticky`, `prefers-reduced-motion` present. Section reveals + marquee-ish "Use for Free / More Templates" blocks.
- **Nav + listing:** Numbered long scroll: `Hey!` → `Services` → `Featured Projects` → `Testimonials` → `Thoughts` → `Let's talk.` Projects are a 4-up named card grid with a one-line category each: `Damas / Agency Framer Template`, `Najm / SaaS Framer Template`, `Kavi / AI Framer Template`, `Sham / Studio Framer Template`. Plus `View All Work`.
- **Adapt:** **This is the closest thing to the target pattern in the whole set.** Cream canvas + one 120px rounded-heavy headline + scattered floating objects at three different scales + a huge counter numeral + named project cards with a one-line category. Swap the objects for paper/cardboard/robot parts and the headline for `造 物 实 验 室`, and it is already the site. Steal the *structure*, not the assets.
- **Don't copy:** The `©2026` mark rendered at 58px as decorative furniture — and, more importantly, a **dark `#111` block per section on a site for children**. Flipping an entire viewport to near-black is a portfolio move; on a shared classroom screen in daylight it turns into a mirror.

### 9. https://akkila.dev — Aleksa Janjic
- **First viewport:** Terminal-flavoured hero: `$ hi, I'm Aleksa` as h1, `AVAILABLE · open to roles & freelance` status pill, inline-italic emphasis inside a large paragraph, `[get in touch]` / `[ls projects/]` as bracketed commands, and an **`~/ask-me.sh` chat widget** (`connected to akkila.dev — ask anything.` with 4 suggested questions).

- **Adapt:** **`3 of 12 visible` + a "list all projects" affordance.** Showing that a filtered view is a *subset* and handing over the escape hatch is honest UI, and it teaches kids the concept of a filter without a single dropdown. Also: `[01] [02] [03]` index numbers on cards.

### 10. https://www.dino-kupinic.dev — Dino Kupinic
- **First viewport:** The **largest display type in the entire set** — h1 `Dino Kupinic` at **148px / weight 500 / letter-spacing −11.1px / line-height 160px**, with h2 `SOFTWARE ENGINEER`. A `<canvas>` (1) sits behind 9 sections.
- **Colour:** Modern `oklch()` tokens: white `oklch(1 0 0)` base, ink `oklch(0.184 0 0)`, and **a set of five pastel chips kept as real tokens** — `oklch(0.932 0.032 255.585)` blue, `oklch(0.954 0.038 75.164)` orange, `oklch(0.962 0.044 156.743)` green, plus violet `oklch(0.558 0.288 302.321)` and red `oklch(0.646 0.222 41.116)` as small text accents. So: **white canvas + a small, deliberately-tokenised pastel tag palette.** This is the single most transferable colour system here for a kids' site.
- **Type:** One custom face, `Raveo`, carrying everything; hierarchy is size + extreme negative tracking (−11.1px at 148px is ~7.5% of the type size).
- **Motion:** Very restrained: **3 animations total**, no GSAP/Lenis detected, `prefers-reduced-motion` in the markup, `sticky` only. A `Cmd/Ctrl K` search field is the main interaction.
- **Nav + listing:** 4-item nav (`PROJECTS / BLOG / ABOUT / USES`), and a **date-first project index**: each row is `September 2026` → `Regress` → one-line description. 14 projects listed on `/projects`, all text, **zero images**. Blog rows add a `?` thumbnail placeholder.
- **Adapt:** (a) **Date-first, text-only project rows** — an archive that reads like a lab notebook index and is cheap to maintain; (b) the **pastel tag token set**: give each subject a colour chip (电路 = blue, 纸模 = orange, 编程 = green) and reuse it everywhere. Both are directly portable.
- **Don't copy:** −11.1px tracking at 148px. At that size it is a designed optical correction for one word on one screen; applied to a Chinese-language headline or a longer phrase it collides letterforms and destroys legibility.

### 12. https://g-dea.com — G's Idea
- **First viewport:** **A literal desktop OS.** Measured: a 347×525 desktop image at (461, 54), then a grid of 13 **app icons rendered as 50×59 buttons paired with 40×48 images**, laid out at x = 522/579/636/693 and rows y = 218 / 281 / 343: `Profile, Projects, Notes, Feedback, Music, Videos, Gallery, Files, Connect`. Chrome includes `☕ 🌜 22:34 14.10.2026`, a 35×35 widget button, and a bottom-right `Views Counter` image.

- **Type:** `Quicksand Medium` — rounded geometric sans — the single most kid-friendly typeface choice in the whole set, plus platform `Arial` fallback for the fake-UI chrome.
- **Motion:** **Zero** detected: `animations: 0`, no keywords at all, no canvas/video/svg. The whole page is 632px tall — exactly one viewport. It is a bitmap + icon grid.
- **Nav + listing:** The **icon grid is the navigation**: projects are app icons, and content opens as windows. No scrolling at all.
- **Adapt:** The **desktop/OS metaphor with a real dock and clock** is enormously legible to a 9–12 year old — they already know that icons open things. This is the strongest *navigational* idea in the set for a "digital lab": 项目 = 桌面图标, 任务卡 = 打开的窗口, 作品 = 文件夹, 说明书 = 便签. Pair it with g-dea's rounded sans and you have something that feels like a place, not a webpage.
- **Don't copy:** Two hard blockers. (1) A single static bitmap desktop means **no text is real** — nothing is selectable, translatable, or indexable, and all that measured text lives in image alt/UI chrome, not content. ## 2. SYNTHESIS

### 2.1 Recurring principles, with evidence counts

**P1 — One oversized statement owns the hero (11/20 measured ≥64px).**
Measured h1 sizes: dino **148px**, oiloil **140px**, majd **120px**, hontran **118.7px**, lixiaolai **89.6px**, heysai **88.5px**, mantis **85px**, aaabad **72px**, ricardo **64px**, thestart 36px + canvas, thinh 36px + WebGL. Negative tracking scales with size: dino −11.1px, oiloil −6.3px, mantis −4.25px, majd −2.4px, lixiaolai −1.79px, heysai −1.77px, brober −1.2px. The hero is a *sentence or a name*, never a value proposition paragraph.

**P2 — Light, low-chroma canvas with only 1–2 saturated accents (12/20 light vs 8/20 dark).**
Light: lixiaolai `oklch(0.985)`, oiloil `#FFF`, hiesther `#FEFCF6`, majd `#FAF7F3`, justinesoulie `#F4F0ED`, brober `#F0F0F0`, dino `#FFF`, aaabad `#FFF`, thinh `#FFF`, akkila (light), mubx (light), thestart (black/`#F6F6F6` flip). Accent discipline is strict: heysai uses **one** colour (`#C9F558`), hontran two (`#FFD900` + orange), hiesther two (blue + yellow), oiloil one blue, eren near-zero. Contrast is carried by **type size and ink-on-paper**, not by colour.

**P3 — Hierarchy comes from an extreme size jump, not weight or colour (9/20).**
The display-to-body ratio is roughly **7–10×**: dino 148→16, oiloil 140→14–16, majd 120→16–18, hontran 118→mono labels, lixiaolai 89.6→small labels, heysai 88.5→16, mantis 85→body, aaabad 72→16, ricardo 64→~18. Weight is often *low* on the big type: lixiaolai and ricardo both set their hero at **weight 400**, and dino at 500.

**P4 — Bilingual / dual-script pairing as a first-class design device (6/20).**
lixiaolai (`中文 · English`, two RSS feeds, CJK serif in the stack), hiesther (`zh-CN`, Noto Sans SC + Noto Serif SC + hand-written English terminal), **nudot** (`WEBDESIGN（ 網頁設計 ）`, full-width parenthetical CJK inside Latin labels, `（ 專注本質的數位實踐 ）`), oiloil (`中 | EN`), ricardo (`EN | ES`), justinesoulie (FR/EN), plus mubx selling `Bilingual (Ar/En)`. **Four of these are CJK↔Latin pairs** — the most directly relevant precedent for MakerX.

**P5 — Motion = smooth-scroll + reveal + one ticker, not autoplay video (9/20 use a recognisable scroll library).**
`lenis` smooth scroll detected on mantis, majd, redoyan, ricardo, erenaygun, thestart, nudot (7); `GSAP` + `ScrollTrigger` on redoyan, nudot, hontran (3); `barba` page transitions on justinesoulie and nudot (2); Framer on majd (1); CSS `ticker`/marquee on lixiaolai, redoyan, nudot, thestart. Crucially, **the most restrained sites are the most confident**: lixiaolai runs **3** animations, brober and aaabadcode **4/3**, dino **3**, g-dea **0** — while the heaviest (nudot, hontran, mantis, thestart) also have the longest pages (10.5k–15.5k px) and the most GPU dependencies.

**P6 — Numbered sections as the navigation spine (7/20).**
lixiaolai `§ 01 ARTICLES / § 02 BOOKS`, mubx `01 The Craft → 07 Contact`, akkila `02FEATURED WORK / 03GET IN TOUCH`, nudot `⟪ 01 // 05 ⟫`, mantis/okkila spec-block counters, erenaygun's named sections. Numbering does two jobs at once: it makes a 10,000px page scannable, and it signals "this is a structured body of work" rather than a pile of links.

**P7 — Project cards carry dense, comparable metadata (8/20).**
The recurring field set: **index number → thumbnail → title → 1–2 category tags → year/duration → one-line outcome**. Richest example mubx (screenshot + number + title + 2 tags + stack + stat + duration + CTA); cleanest mantis (image + title + 2 tags); most minimal brober (`tag / title / one sentence`); most archival dino (date → title → one line, text only); plus counters like akkila's `3 of 12 visible` and mantis's `Selected works 6`.

**P8 — Status chrome and self-description as trust signals (7/20).**
Live clocks (mantis `New York City 10:37:24`, g-dea `☕🌜 22:34 14.10.2026`), self-declared colophons (lixiaolai *"SET IN Newsreader, NOTO SERIF & INTER · 14 OCT 2026"*; brober *"Colophon: Geist Mono and Geist Sans. By Vercel."*), honest metrics (lixiaolai `~2 articles / week`, `14 volumes, in rotation`; akkila `3 of 12 visible`; brober `replies in < 24h`), and availability pills (akkila `AVAILABLE · open to roles`, mubx `Accepting new projects`). These small, verifiable, self-aware details do more for credibility than award badges — and they map perfectly onto a parent's questions about a course.

**P9 — Accessibility is inconsistently honoured (5/20 have `prefers-reduced-motion`; 6/20 have a scroll-dependent or JS-dependent reading path).**
Present on thinh, majd, dino, aaabadcode, nudot. Absent on the heaviest sites (mantis, hontran, thestart, ricardochance, erenaygun). And several sites **fail the basic text test**: heysai's paragraph body literally contains every word twice (`I I build build software software`), hontran's h1 is 36 separate lines (`A / W / A / R / D / - / W…`), g-dea ships one bitmap with no real content text, and mantis's case studies return near-empty text until scrolled (13,132px of height, almost no extractable copy). g-dea additionally gates mobile behind *"Go back to portrait mode."*

### 2.2 What NOT to do for a 9–12 audience

1. **Full-viewport WebGL/canvas at first paint.** Direct evidence: mantis (1280×800 canvas at y=0), thestart (two stacked full-viewport canvases), justinesoulie (1268×632 canvas as the *only* first-viewport content), hontran (5 canvases), nudot (4 canvases + 10 videos). On a school Chromebook or a 3-year-old tablet this is a blank box; it also means no readable content until the GPU context is granted.
2. **Preloaders that gate content.** redoyan `LOADING 0%` → `WELCOME`; thinh a `01…50` counter; heysai `CALIBRATING 99%`; hiesther `Press Enter to Launch`. A child who sees 0% assumes it's broken; a counter is a promise the network can break.
3. **Scroll-hijacking smooth scroll.** `lenis` on ~7 sites. It breaks trackpad momentum, keyboard paging, `Ctrl+F`, and browser scrollbar expectations. On a shared classroom device it reads as "the page won't scroll properly."
4. **Custom cursors and cursor-follow effects.** erenaygun, justinesoulie, nudot all ship `cursor:` overrides. Most 9–12s arrive on touch devices where a custom cursor does not exist — so the effect is invisible at best, and on hybrid laptops it hides the pointer a child needs.
5. **Per-word / per-line text reveal on body copy.** heysai's actual DOM text is `I I build build software software by by treating treating AI AI…`. This is the clearest accessibility failure in the set: doubled reading load, broken screen readers, broken copy-paste, broken translation.
6. **Vertical letter-stacking display type.** hontran's h1 real text is 36 one-character lines. It is unreadable to a 9-year-old and to assistive tech, and it breaks search.
7. **Dark + neon-glow "AI bro portfolio" clichés.** heysai `#070707` + `#C9F558`, redoyan `#000` + violet `#A87CFF`/`#FB8DFF` glow, erenaygun `#101010` + Cinzel Roman bust, ricardochance `#0D0718`, hontran `#121110` + `#FFD900`. Fine for attracting engineering recruiters; wrong for a room of 10-year-olds and for a parent co-viewing on a bright screen.
8. **Extreme negative letter-spacing on impulse.** dino's −11.1px at 148px is an optical correction for one Latin word. Applied to a Chinese headline or a longer phrase it collides letterforms. Use `clamp()` + modest tracking instead (hontran's `clamp()` usage is the good half of that site).
9. **Decorative text below any contrast floor.** nudot sets 72 elements at `rgba(255,255,255,0.25)`. Decorative or not, this fails accessibility and looks broken on a projector.
10. **In-group jargon as the primary register.** `AWWWARDS JURY · 2× INDEPENDENT OF THE YEAR NOMINEE`, `white-label development`, `mutation testing`, `bonding curve token trading`, `$ ls -al /projects/`, `/rss.xml`. Meaningless to a child and to most parents.
11. **Navigation hidden behind hover/click disclosure.** thestart's header exposed **0** links to my probe — you must discover `Menu`/`Talk`; erenaygun hides behind anchor jumps; g-dea hides everything behind an icon grid with no text. A parent looking for 课程/项目/报名 must not have to hunt.
12. **Content that only exists after scrolling.** mantis's 13,132px case study yielded almost no extractable text; several sites use `sticky` + `clip-path` reveals. If it isn't in the DOM reading order, it isn't there.
13. **Very long scroll with no map.** Page heights measured: hontran 15,520px, thestart 13,455px, mantis 12,690px (and 13,132px on the case study), ricardochance 11,994px, nudot 10,546px. Pair that with no sticky section indicator and a child loses their place immediately. (lixiaolai's `§01 §02` numbering and akkila's/mubx's numbered headers are the fix.)
14. **Awards and vanity metrics as credibility.** 19 awards (hontran), 26 FWA/Cannes figures (mantis), a 24-logo client wall and award wall (thestart), `10M+ requests/day` (heysai). Parents of 9–12s care about *what my child will make, how long it takes, what it costs, and whether it's safe* — mubx's FAQ and outcome stats (`1 week`, `60fps`) are the useful half of that genre.
15. **Gate or exclude mobile.** g-dea literally instructs *"Go back to portrait mode."* Never.

### 2.3 The 3 most relevant models for "light/airy + big clear type + playful floating objects + project-card navigation + scroll storytelling"

**① [majd-portfolio.framer.website](https://majd-portfolio.framer.website) — the structural template.**
It is the only site that hits all five criteria in the first viewport simultaneously, and it does so with **zero WebGL**: cream `#FAF7F3`, a **120px/800 headline with −2.4px tracking**, and **three image objects floating at three different scales around the type** (124×124 top-left, 220×251 centre, 173×173 right — measured), plus a giant `©2026` counter at 58px as compositional weight. Navigation is a named project-card grid (`Damas / Agency Framer Template`) where every card carries a one-line category, and the section spine is `Hey! → Services → Featured Projects → Testimonials → Thoughts → Let's talk.` Motion is Framer + `lenis`: gentle scroll reveal, low GPU cost, `prefers-reduced-motion` present. **Take:** the hero composition (one heavy headline + 3 scattered objects + one giant numeral), the card metadata shape, and the hard cream/`#111` section alternation *at a smaller scale* — never a full dark viewport on a children's site.

**② [oiloil.org](https://www.oiloil.org) — the interaction-and-objects template.**
White canvas, `#1A1A1A` ink, **140px/700 headline `Oil® makes things.` with a real 331×441 object image overlapping the words** — the "playful floating object" hero achieved with a single image and no canvas at all. Under it, a numbered product list (`01 Wolfcha`, one-line pitch, `Play the game →`) gives the project-card navigation, and the `Products / Consulting & learning / Skills / Articles / About` split shows how to separate *things to do* from *things to read*. Its decisive advantage is the **interaction gallery**: `Hover`, `Spring`, `Glass` each demonstrated with explicit descriptions, `Compare`/`Replay` controls and a `blur: on/off` toggle. For a creative-technology course this is a ready-made pattern for teaching *what an interaction is* — and it proves the site practises its own curriculum. It is also the **only top-3 candidate with a visible `中 | EN` language switcher**.

**③ [hiesther.me](https://hiesther.me) — the "digital lab" framing template, in Chinese.**
The only zh-CN site here whose *structure* is already a lab. First viewport is a terminal panel (`$ whoami` → `> ESTHER不二`, `$ cat about.md`, `$ open esther-os.app`, `Press Enter to Launch`) with three app tabs below it — `01主页 / 02作品集 / 03我的OS` — and a 5-link project ticker across the top. Palette is cream `#FEFCF6` with exactly two accents (blue `#2B7FD8`, yellow `#F4D758`), and the type stack pairs `Noto Sans SC` + `Noto Serif SC` with **`Caveat` handwriting** and `Fira Code` — a direct precedent for "serious Chinese type + a hand-drawn, kid-friendly voice" in the same page. It demonstrates that a Chinese-language personal site can carry personality through *system framing* rather than decoration. **Take:** tabs-as-apps, the console panel as an "about" block, the marquee link index, the mono/handwriting split. **Leave:** its 209 live animations and its launch gate — reproduce the *idea* at perhaps a tenth of the motion density.

## 3. Failures, unreachable URLs, and uncertainty

### 3.1 Hard failures
**None.** All 20 URLs returned HTTP 200 and were read. No DNS failures, no 404s, no timeouts, no bot-blocks (no CAPTCHA/Cloudflare interstitial was encountered).

### 3.2 Tool-level failures I had to work around
- **`web_fetch` refused 9 cross-origin redirects** and returned an error until I retried the canonical host: `mantis.works → www.mantis.works`, `brober.xyz → www.brober.xyz`, `aaabadcode.com → www.aaabadcode.com`, `redoyanulhaque.me → www.redoyanulhaque.me`, `dino-kupinic.dev → www.dino-kupinic.dev`, `erenaygun.com → www.erenaygun.com`, `hontran.dev → www.hontran.dev`, `oiloil.org → www.oiloil.org`, `ricardochance.com → www.ricardochance.com`.
- **8 sites were JS shells and yielded no usable content as plain text**, requiring headless rendering: **hiesther.me** (returned a single title string — a `<div id="root">` Vite SPA), **majd-portfolio.framer.website** (returned only the word "Majd"), **heysai.dev** (returned only "Sai"), **redoyanulhaque.me** (title only), **thinh.io.vn** (title only), **g-dea.com** (only the portrait-mode notice), **dino-kupinic.dev** (title only), **nudot.com.tw** (title only). For these, everything reported comes from headless rendering, not from the text fetch.
- **mantis.works case-study pages defeated text extraction even after rendering:** `/work/shopify-dot-dev-26` reported `scrollHeight: 13132` but its extracted body text contained only the header chrome (nav, NYC clock, `hey@mantis.works`). The content is scroll-revealed. I therefore describe Mantis's *homepage* and its *card* pattern and make no claim about its case-study structure.
- **Vision was unavailable, so I never saw a pixel.** `modlens_read_image` failed on every call with `Every configured vision provider failed … OpenAI-compatible API error 401: unauthorized` (and codex-cli provider 401 on `wss://api.openai.com/v1/responses`), and `read_image` is not usable because the running model declares no image input. I captured six hero screenshots to `shots/` in this workspace (`majd-hero.png`, `hiesther-hero.png`, `oiloil-hero.png`, `gdea-hero.png`, `justine-hero.png`, `aaabad-hero.png`) but could not inspect them myself — **they are unverified**, and any agent with vision should check them before quoting my hero descriptions as visual fact.

### 3.3 Known uncertainty in my own measurements
- **All "first viewport" claims are at 1268×632** (headless Chromium default; one pass at 1440×900 for screenshots). Element positions shift at other sizes, so treat the geometry as evidence of *arrangement and relative scale*, not of an exact layout.
- **My `rive` keyword matches are unreliable** — the string is a substring of "drive"/"derive". **I make no claim anywhere that any site uses Rive.**
- **`sticky`, `video`, `marquee` and `mix-blend` keyword hits are noisy** (class names, unrelated prose). The trustworthy stack signals are `gsap`, `ScrollTrigger`, `lenis`/`Lenis`, `barba`, `THREE`, and `data-framer`.
- **Markup scanning badly under-reports motion on SPA/Vite sites.** hiesther.me showed only `sticky` in its markup yet had **209 live animations** running. Conversely, some counts (lixiaolai 3, g-dea 0) are genuinely low. Treat the animation counts as measured at one moment during/just after load, not as totals.
- **Two palette artefacts are not brand choices.** `rgb(0, 0, 238)` (`#0000EE`) appears with high counts on majd, hiesther, erenaygun and nudot: this is the browser's **default unstyled link colour** in my headless context, not a design accent — I have excluded it from every "accent" claim. Similarly, **majd's measured 12px body font is Framer's default root size**, not an intentional value.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` presence ≠ a good reduced-motion experience.** I only verified that the string appears in the served CSS/HTML on 5 sites. I did **not** emulate the media feature, so I cannot say what those sites actually do when it is active.
- **I did not test touch/mobile at all.** My claims that custom cursors and `barba` transitions "don't work on touchscreens" are reasoned from the technology, not observed on a device. Likewise, I did not test keyboard navigation, screen-reader output, or actual performance/CPU cost — the GPU-cost and bandwidth concerns in §2.2 are inference from asset counts (canvas/video counts, page heights), not from profiling.
- **Text-colour histograms are element counts, not area.** A colour with a high element count may occupy very little screen area (e.g. per-character spans), so I used them to identify *which* colours exist and roughly their role, not their visual weight.
